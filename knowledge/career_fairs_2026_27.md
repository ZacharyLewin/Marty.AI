# McGill career fairs, 2026–27

Career fairs at McGill are run by several groups: CaPS (the central Career Planning Service), the faculty career offices (Desautels, Engineering, Education), and student associations (AUS, SUS, MSAA). The master list is on the CaPS career fairs page. Dates below were checked on 2 November 2026. Always tell students to re-check the official page before they go.

## Ground rules for every fair
- **Bring your McGill ID.** Only current McGill students and recent alumni get in, and the ID is checked at the door.
- **Bring paper CVs.** Recruiters still collect them.
- Most fairs need **no pre-registration** for students. You just walk in. The MSAA fair is the exception (Google Form).
- CaPS publishes a how-to guide: https://www.mcgill.ca/caps/files/caps/how_to_prepare_for_career_fairs_july_2022.pdf
- Want help choosing fairs or prepping? Book a CaPS advisor through myFuture, or use drop-in advising: https://www.mcgill.ca/caps/students/services/advising

## What each fair is good for
- **Grad & Professional Schools Fair.** Law, med, public policy, management, and master's and PhD programs across the sciences and humanities. Useful for any U2/U3 weighing grad school against a job, and it's free intel on admissions and funding.
- **Jaclyn Fisher.** Desautels' flagship fair: banks, consulting, consumer goods and tech. It's open to every faculty, so Arts and Science students go too.
- **TechFair.** Engineering, software, hardware and tech firms. Also worth it for CS, maths and stats students.
- **Social Impact & Summer Jobs Fair.** NGOs, the public sector, camps and summer roles. The best fair for first and second years.
- **Macdonald Campus fair.** Agriculture, environment, food science and nutrition employers.

## Insider tips
- Walk the room once before you talk to anyone, then hit your top three while you're warm.
- Ask about entry-level programs and timelines. "When does your 2027 recruiting open?" gets more useful answers than "Are you hiring?"
- Talk to at least one employer in a field you'd never considered. Recruiters remember curious students.
- First-years: go anyway. CaPS says it's never too early.
