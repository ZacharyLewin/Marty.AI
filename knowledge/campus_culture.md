# McGill culture: traditions, places and slang

This is the stuff nobody tells you at orientation but everyone knows by November.

## Traditions
- **Frosh.** A student-run, four-day orientation party at the end of August, run by faculty societies (Arts Frosh, Science Frosh, Engineering Frosh…) and by non-faculty groups. Architecture students join Engineering Frosh. There are alternatives for people who want something different: **Outdoors Frosh** (run by the McGill Outdoors Club: climbing, hiking, cycling), **Rad Frosh**, and dry options. Frosh is separate from official Orientation and Discover McGill. It's for new undergrads only, and every event is opt-in.
- **OAP (Open Air Pub).** A McGill tradition since the early 1980s. It began as an engineering students' barbecue and is now a student-run outdoor pub on lower campus with live student bands, burgers and cheap beer. It runs at the start of the fall semester (add/drop season) and again after finals in April. Students call it "The Best Place on Earth". You need McGill ID, and the queue snakes up the Y-intersection.
- **Faculty Olympics.** Faculties compete in ridiculous team events in early fall.
- **Broomball on Lower Field.** One of Engineering's oldest winter traditions.
- **The Iron Rink.** An outdoor skating rink on lower campus in winter.
- **Faculty pubs.** Arts has **BdA** (Bar des Arts), a weekly Arts pub. Engineering has **Blues Pub**. They're open to everyone, but each faculty's crowd dominates.

## Places
- **Gerts.** The student bar in the basement of the SSMU building, around since 1974. Cheap drinks, clubs rent it for fundraisers, and the saying is "Gerts 'til it hurts." (Gerts Café runs during the day.)
- **Shatner / the SSMU building.** Officially the University Centre, 3480 McTavish. Home to SSMU, Gerts, the **SSMU Ballroom** (room 301, where most career fairs happen) and Activities Night.
- **The Y-intersection.** The fork in the main path up from the Roddick Gates. Everyone meets there.
- **Roddick Gates.** The main gates on Sherbrooke. The classic meeting point and photo spot.
- **Lower Field.** The big green in the middle of downtown campus. OAP, sunbathing in September, broomball in winter.
- **Leacock.** The Arts building. Leacock 132 is the giant lecture hall every Arts student ends up in.
- **McLennan-Redpath.** The main library complex, where finals season is spent.
- **Burnside.** Maths and stats (Burnside Hall). You'll learn the elevators.
- **Brown Building.** Student Services: CaPS, the Student Wellness Hub, and more.
- **Milton-Parc / "the ghetto".** The student neighbourhood east of campus. Most first-years off residence end up here or on the Plateau.
- **Mount Royal.** Right behind campus. Locals say "the mountain", and the run up from Peel is a rite of passage.

## Slang and McGill-isms
- **U0 / U1 / U2 / U3.** Year levels. U0 is the foundation year for students coming from outside Quebec's CEGEP system. U1 is the first year of a 3-year degree.
- **Add/drop.** The first couple of weeks of term, when you can change courses freely.
- **Minerva.** The registration and records system. **myCourses.** The course site (assignments, grades). **myFuture.** The career platform.
- **Martlet.** McGill's mascot bird (a heraldic bird with no feet). Teams are the **Redbirds** (men) and **Martlets** (women).
- **The Daily / The Tribune / The Bull & Bear.** Student papers. The Bull & Bear is the Desautels/management paper.
- **"Ghetto".** Milton-Parc (see above).
- **"Leacock 132".** Shorthand for a big intro lecture.
- **Bilingual city, English campus.** Classes are in English, but you can submit written work in French. Montréal life runs in French, so learn your *bonjour-hi*.

## How Marty should use this
Use the culture to make answers feel like they come from a friend who's been here a while. Never use it to replace facts. If a tradition's date or status matters ("is OAP happening this week?"), say you'd check the live events feed or SSMU's channels.
